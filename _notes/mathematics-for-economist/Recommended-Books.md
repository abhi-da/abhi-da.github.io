The following list contains books whose tone and language I personally liked. They may not suit everyone, so I recommend giving a few of them a read to find the ones you can follow easily. Every author writes from their own perspective, and needs vary from reader to reader—so be selective when choosing your textbooks.

---

## How to Think Like a Mathematician

[How to Think Like a Mathematician: A Companion to Undergraduate Mathematics](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/6220915-how-to-think-like-a-mathematician)  
This book is great for anyone being introduced to rigorous mathematics for the first time. It ignites curiosity, explains why math is important, and shows how to express thoughts mathematically. I strongly suggest reading at least the first chapter.

---

## Logic

[Mathematical Proofs: A Transition to Advanced Mathematics](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/2461863.Mathematical_Proofs)  
I loved the simplicity of the language here—it felt as if someone were sitting beside me and explaining the concepts directly.

---

## Analysis

[Principles of Mathematical Analysis](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/292079.Principles_of_Mathematical_Analysis) (often called *Baby Rudin*)  
This is my personal favorite because of its clear and precise language.

People also frequently recommend Terence Tao's analysis books:
* [Analysis I](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/502785.Analysis_I)
* [Analysis II](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/2578776-analysis-ii)

---

## Linear Algebra

[Linear Algebra and Its Applications](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/179699.Linear_Algebra_and_Its_Applications) by Gilbert Strang  
This is the main linear algebra textbook I have worked through. 

Gilbert Strang's full lecture series is available on YouTube:
* [Gilbert Strang - Linear Algebra Lectures](https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PL49CF3715CB9EF31D&si=GQkAb8xWQyCxBdtL)

---

## Calculus

[Thomas' Calculus](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/818077.Thomas_Calculus)  
A solid resource with a large collection of exercises.

Alternatively, you can pick up any standard economics mathematics textbook, such as:
* [Mathematics for Economists](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/130107.Mathematics_for_Economists) by Simon & Blume

---

> **Note:** I strongly recommend reading *How to Think Like a Mathematician* first. It will give you a better idea of how to select textbooks and navigate between topics effectively.

You can also find a broader curated list here: [Awesome Math Series](https://github.com/rossant/awesome-math)
